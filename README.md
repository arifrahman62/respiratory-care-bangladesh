# Oxygen Concentrators for Home Care in Bangladesh

When a family member needs long-term oxygen therapy, the choice usually comes down to oxygen cylinders or an oxygen concentrator. For home use, concentrators have become the practical option for thousands of families across Bangladesh. Here is what you should know.

## How an Oxygen Concentrator Works

Unlike a cylinder that stores a fixed amount of oxygen, a concentrator pulls in room air, filters out nitrogen, and delivers concentrated oxygen continuously. Plug it in, and it produces oxygen as long as it has power — no refills, no delivery trucks, no running out at midnight.

## Concentrator vs Cylinder

**Running cost:** A concentrator's main cost is electricity. Cylinders need regular refills, and refill logistics in Dhaka traffic — or outside Dhaka — can be a real burden.

**Safety:** Concentrators do not store oxygen under high pressure, which removes the risks associated with cylinder handling and storage.

**Portability:** 5-liter stationary units suit home use; portable concentrators with batteries allow limited mobility for patients who still move around.

## What Size Do You Need?

Oxygen prescriptions are measured in liters per minute (LPM). 5L concentrators cover most home-therapy prescriptions (1–5 LPM). Patients needing higher flows require 10L units. Always follow the doctor's prescribed flow rate — more oxygen is not better.

## Buying Tips

- Check the oxygen purity specification (typically 90–96% at rated flow).
- Ask about noise level — the machine may run all night in a bedroom.
- Confirm warranty and servicing: filters and sieve beds need periodic replacement.
